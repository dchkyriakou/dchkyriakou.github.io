A Parkinson's signal in the wrist pulse

Digital Biomarkers from Wrist Photoplethysmography for Parkinson's Disease Discrimination Using Machine Learning. Diploma thesis.

Supervisor: Prof. Leontios Hadjileontiadis · 30 ECTS · Electrical & Computer Engineering, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki

Download CV (PDF)Texas A&M poster (PDF)

Abstract

Wrist photoplethysmography (PPG) recorded in daily life is dominated by motion and poor contact. This thesis builds an end-to-end Python pipeline on free-living PPG and accelerometer data from the PPMI cohort: 344 participants with Parkinson's disease, prodromal participants and healthy controls. A four-stage signal-quality gate (motion, waveform shape, template correlation ≥ 0.70 and a post-motion buffer) reduced 249.3 million candidate 30-second windows to 101.3 million clean windows (40.64%). Twenty-seven pulse-rate-variability indices, computed at 30-second and 5-minute scales by day and night, gave 594 features per participant. Logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost and support vector machines were evaluated with participant-level nested cross-validation and Nadeau–Bengio corrected tests. Pulse-rate-variability features improved Parkinson's-versus-control ROC-AUC by 0.298 to 0.323 over age and sex in all four models (p < 0.05), and the gain was stable across sensitivity analyses. No added value was shown for prodromal participants versus controls, a result reported as such.

How the pipeline was built

The choices behind each step: the problem, what I decided, and why.

SynchronisationTwo sensors, two sample rates, gaps in the recording
Problem
PPG at about 30 Hz and accelerometer at 100 Hz, recorded in daily life with gaps.
Decision
Both onto a common 30 Hz grid. A gap longer than 0.5 s starts a new segment; interpolation only inside continuous segments; filtering per segment, not per window. Local time from the recorded UTC offset, with each day anchored 07:00 to 07:00.
Why
Interpolating across a gap would invent signal, and filtering every window separately would put edge transients into every window.
Windows30 s long, 15 s step
Problem
Choosing a window long enough for pulse-rate variability, short enough to fit between movements.
Decision
30-second windows with a 15-second step.
Why
Periods of rest in daily life are short.
Quality gate249,275,571 windows in, 101,310,466 out (40.64%)
Problem
Free-living wrist PPG is dominated by motion and poor contact.
Decision
  1. Motion: Var(|a|) ≤ 0.025 g²; reject when at least 10% of |a| power lies in 3.5–7.5 Hz (the Parkinsonian tremor band) and the dominant |a| and PPG frequencies are within 0.5 Hz of each other.
  2. Shape: skewness in [−1.5, 4.0], kurtosis in [1.2, 20].
  3. Template: mean correlation with the median pulse ≥ 0.70.
  4. Buffer: 45 s after motion.
Why
The buffer allows haemodynamic recovery after movement. The tremor rule targets the frequency band of Parkinsonian tremor specifically.
Result
40.64% of windows kept; about 55% of night windows were clean against about 13% by day.
Beat detectionSub-sample timing from a 30 Hz signal
Problem
At 30 Hz, beat timing is uncertain by ±16.65 ms.
Decision
Peaks above the 40th percentile with prominence ≥ 10% of the range, refined by parabolic 3-point interpolation. Inter-beat intervals kept within 333–2000 ms (30–180 bpm); a rolling-median filter removes intervals more than 25% from the median of 5 neighbours. A window needs ≤ 1 removal, ≥ 15 clean intervals and ≥ 14 valid pairs.
Why
Interpolating the peak recovers timing finer than the sampling grid.
Features27 indices × night/day × 11 statistics = 594
Problem
Overlapping windows are not independent samples.
Decision
6 PRV indices per 30 s window; 21 indices on reconstructed 5-minute segments (intervals to a cubic spline at 4 Hz, Welch with 64 s Hann windows, 50% overlap, detrending; LF 0.04–0.15 Hz, HF 0.15–0.40 Hz). Summarised per participant: 27 indices × night/day × 11 statistics = 594 features.
Why
The participant, not the window, is the unit of analysis.
Parameters without ground truthNo ECG reference to tune against
Problem
No ECG ground truth in the free-living data.
Decision
Parameters chosen by day-to-day repeatability, ICC(3,1), on a 30-person development subset (10 per group).
Limitation
ICC measures repeatability, not accuracy, and the thesis states this.
ValidationNested cross-validation, corrected tests
Problem
A small cohort, many features, and training sets that overlap across folds.
Decision
5 outer folds stratified at participant level × 10 repeats (seeds 42–51). 3 inner folds for feature selection (drop features with more than 20% missing or zero variance, prune Spearman |ρ| > 0.90, SelectKBest with k ∈ {3, 5, 10, 20}), hyperparameters and decision threshold. Balanced class weights; age and sex as a parallel baseline branch.
Why
Nadeau–Bengio corrected t-tests, because the training sets overlap.
Results+0.298 to +0.323 ROC-AUC; an honest negative too
Parkinson's vs controls
ROC-AUC +0.298 to +0.323 over age and sex in all four models (p < 0.05), stable across sensitivity analyses (residualised on age and sex, correlation filters at 0.80 and 0.70, L1 selection).
Prodromal vs controls
No added value shown, reported as such (PPMI prodromal heterogeneity).
Most selected
HF in normalised units and LF/HF variability (88–94% of runs), SD1/SD2 extremes, and nocturnal entropy for the prodromal group.
